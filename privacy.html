Formcast

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 10, 2026

In short

  • Formcast has no servers of its own. Your training data lives on your device. The only exception is what you yourself choose to post in an optional friends circle, which is stored in iCloud (see "Friends circles" below).
  • You connect exactly one fitness data source — Garmin Connect or Fitbit — not both. Its session token or OAuth token, plus your AI provider API key, are stored only in your device's Keychain — never on a server we run, because we don't run one.
  • The only network calls Formcast makes are directly to Garmin Connect, Google's Health API (for Fitbit), Apple Health, the AI provider you choose and pay for, and — only if you use friends circles — Apple's iCloud. Never to a Formcast server, because there is none.
  • Friends circles are optional. Sleep, weight and coach texts are never shared; pace, heart rate, a route and similar details only go along when you tick them on an activity you post (or for a circle where you switched on automatic posting).
  • A photo only leaves your phone when you use "Routine from a photo" in the Gym: it then goes once to the AI provider you chose, to read the exercises.
  • Formcast reads Apple Health data, and writes to it only when you record a workout yourself in the app.
  • Nothing is sold, shared with advertisers, or used for tracking. There are no ads and no analytics SDKs in this app.

What Formcast is

Formcast is a native iPhone/iPad app that reads your training and health data from Garmin Connect, Fitbit (via the Google Health API), and/or Apple Health, and uses an AI model to turn it into a daily readiness score, coaching messages, and training plans. You can also record a workout live in the app — GPS route, distance, pace and heart rate. It's built and operated by an independent developer, not a company — there is no backend server, no user accounts, and no centralized database of user data.

Data Formcast reads

Garmin Connect — if you sign in, Formcast requests your activities, daily metrics (steps, resting heart rate, stress, Body Battery), sleep, and HRV directly from Garmin's own servers using your Garmin Connect email, password, and 2-factor code. Formcast stores only the resulting session token (not your password) in your device's Keychain.

Fitbit (via Google Health API) — if you choose Fitbit instead, you sign in with your Google account through Google's own consent screen, and Formcast requests read-only access to your activity/workout, sleep, and step data through the Google Health API. Formcast never sees your Google password — only an OAuth access token and refresh token, which are stored in your device's Keychain. Formcast's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: that data is used only to show you your own training and readiness information inside the app, is never sold, and is never used for advertising.

Apple Health — if you grant access, Formcast reads workouts (including their route and heart-rate samples), steps, heart rate, resting heart rate, HRV, sleep stages, active energy, distance, respiratory rate, blood-oxygen and VO₂ max through HealthKit — either as your main data source (for an Apple Watch or iPhone) or to top up Garmin or Fitbit data. This data stays on your device; the only part that ever leaves it is the short text summary of recent numbers sent to the AI provider you chose when you use the AI coach. Per Apple's rules, HealthKit data is never used for advertising or marketing and is never sold.

Data Formcast writes

Apple Health — Formcast writes to Health in exactly one situation: when you tap "Start recording" and record a workout live in the app. That workout — its distance, route, duration, and active energy — is saved to Apple Health the same way any other workout-recording app would save it, so it also shows up in the Fitness app. Formcast never writes to Health at any other time, and never touches any Health data beyond that one workout.

Friends circles (optional)

The Friends tab lets you form small circles with friends. Nothing is sent to iCloud until you agree to the circle rules in that tab, and you can stay out of circles entirely. Circles run on Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) — Formcast still has no server of its own, and the developer cannot read what is in a circle: it lives in the iCloud accounts of the people in it.

What goes into a circle

  • A nickname you choose and a random member ID (not your name, email or Apple ID).
  • Every week and month, automatically: your number of sessions, minutes and distance, your streak of weeks that you met your own weekly session goal, and that goal. If you switch it on, a small green/yellow/red readiness dot as well (only the colour — never the numbers behind it).
  • Activities you post yourself, one at a time — or, only if you switch on Post automatically for a circle, your new activities of the sports you pick there (or only the ones that set a personal record): the title, sport, date, time, duration and distance. If the activity set a personal record (for example "fastest 5 km"), that line goes along. Per post you can add more, and only if you tick it: pace or speed, average and maximum heart rate, calories, elevation gain, the exercises with sets, reps and weight (strength), and a route on a map. A route is reduced to about 100 points and, unless you turn that off, its first and last 200 metres are left out so it doesn't show where you live. A preview in the app shows exactly what friends will see before you post.
  • Your likes (emoji) and comments, with your nickname, and the circle's name and goals set by its owner.
  • For the monthly bingo and the weekly look-back, per month: your sessions, minutes and distance per week, whether you met your own goal that week, and which bingo squares you have (they tick themselves from your own training, such as "a run of 10 km" or "train before 7:00", or you tick a few yourself). Not the activities behind them.
  • Once a week, the phone of one member writes a short look-back at the previous week for the whole circle (totals, who met their own goal, a few light-hearted awards) and stores it in the circle, so everyone sees the same one. Its text is written by the AI on that phone (Apple's on-device model: nothing leaves the phone); only if the circle's owner switches on Allow cloud AI may a member's own AI provider write it, and that provider then receives the members' nicknames and week numbers. Otherwise a fixed text is used. The owner can also switch on a playful "roast" tone.

Ideas for a comment and the text of a "nudge" for a friend are made only for you, follow the same AI rule, are never stored, and are only posted if you send them yourself.

Never shared with a circle: sleep, weight, the coach's texts, your notes, your journal, or anything from Garmin, Fitbit or Apple Health that you didn't post yourself.

Who can see it People in the same circle see what is listed above, under your nickname. A circle is created in the owner's iCloud and joined through an invitation link; anyone who has the link can join, so share it only with people you trust. Circle content is stored by Apple in iCloud under Apple's own terms and privacy policy, and members can of course screenshot or remember what they see.

Notifications To tell you about news (a like, a comment, a shared activity, a goal reached) Formcast uses iCloud's silent change notifications and local notifications on your device, and checks when the app opens and in the background. If you agree to the circle rules, the app asks for notification permission; you can refuse it and still use circles.

Rules, reporting and blocking Comments are checked on your device for insults and similar content before they are posted. You can report a comment: that opens a pre-filled email to the developer with the circle's name, the author's nickname and member ID, the comment's ID and its text — it is only sent if you press send in your mail app. You can block a member (stored only on your device, it hides their content for you) and delete your own comments; a circle's owner can delete any comment or delete the circle. Reports are read by the developer, who cannot open a circle's content (it is in the members' iCloud accounts), and are acted on where possible — for example by contacting the circle's owner.

Leaving and deleting In Settings → Friends → My circles you can leave a circle, which removes your status, posted activities, likes and comments from it, or, as the owner, delete the circle for everyone in it. Leaving also removes your monthly bingo and week numbers and any weekly look-back your phone wrote. Stopping a post removes it together with its likes and comments. "Delete everything" in Settings also leaves all your circles (and deletes the circles you made, for everyone in them), so what you shared there is removed too; if iCloud can't be reached at that moment the app tells you, and you can leave them later yourself. "Wipe local data" does not touch your circles. Apple's iCloud keeps a circle's data until it is removed.

Where your data goes

All synced data is stored locally on your device, using Apple's on-device SwiftData storage. It is not uploaded to any server operated by Formcast, because none exists.

Things you enter yourself are stored on your device in the same way: your aim, goal, focus sports and notes from the PT tab's intake, workouts you add or record, and the exercises, sets, reps and weights you log for strength sessions. Gym routines you prepare (exercises, sets, reps, rest, supersets), the numbers you tick off during a gym workout, your body-weight entries and whether you're bulking, cutting or maintaining are stored on your device as well; when you use the AI coach, the exercises, sets and weights of your recent workouts, your recent weigh-ins and that phase are part of the short summary sent to the AI provider you chose. The small demonstration pictures of the built-in exercises are bundled inside the app (public-domain free-exercise-db) — no image is downloaded. The rest timer can show a local notification on your device when your rest is over; nothing about it leaves the phone. "Wipe local data" and "Delete everything" remove routines and weigh-ins too. When you chat with the PT, the earlier turns of that chat (up to twelve, answers shortened) are sent to the AI provider you chose together with your new question, so follow-ups make sense; starting a new chat stops sending the old ones. Planned strength days may list exercises (suggested by the AI or by a built-in routine) which stay on your device. Formcast also keeps a backup copy of what you created yourself — routines, weigh-ins, workouts you logged or recorded (with their exercises, sets and weights), your journal, plans and goals — as a file in the app's Documents folder (visible in the Files app under Formcast → Backups, and part of your iPhone's own backup); it never leaves your device unless you share it yourself, and synced Garmin, Fitbit and Apple Health data is not part of it. If the app's database can't be opened after an update, the old files are moved into that same folder instead of being deleted. The journal ("How was yesterday?") holds the tags you tap (caffeine, alcohol, illness …) per day; the PT tab compares them with your next-morning HRV, resting heart rate and sleep on your device, and when you use the AI coach the last two weeks of tags and any pattern found are part of the short summary sent to the AI provider you chose. Strength progress (records, estimated 1RM, sets per muscle group) is calculated on your device; recent records and weekly set counts are part of that same summary. The lock-screen / Dynamic Island view of a running gym workout and the home-screen "Today" widget show your own numbers on your device; the widget reads the same snapshot the Apple Watch app uses, kept in a shared Keychain group on your iPhone. The suggestions on the PT tab's "Next week" card (what to lift next time, how long to run, when to take an easier week) are calculated on your device from this data; that part never leaves your phone.

Also on your device only: the shoes you add (name, kilometres, limit) and which runs count for them (part of the backup file); your personal records and the month and year overview (a share picture of it only goes where you send it); the race-week screen (its carbohydrate advice uses your latest weigh-in, its checklist is remembered per race) and the form forecast; the reminders when your week goal is at risk or when nothing has synced for a day (local notifications). The route heatmap keeps simplified routes (about 150 points each) in a file on your device; routes of Garmin activities it doesn't have yet are fetched from Garmin with your existing sign-in. The voice coach speaks on your device with Apple's speech synthesis, from today's planned session and your live distance and pace; nothing is sent. The camera is used only when you take a photo for "Routine from a photo".

Routine from a photo If you use this in the Gym, the photo you take or pick is made smaller (about 1,600 pixels) and sent once, directly from your device, to the AI provider you configured, together with the list of built-in exercise names, so it can read the exercises, sets and reps. Formcast doesn't keep the photo; that provider's own privacy policy applies to the request. Apple's on-device model can't read photos, so this needs your own API key.

When you use an AI coaching feature (daily briefing, nudges, activity insights, Q&A, weekly review, or plan generation), Formcast sends a compact, text-only summary of the relevant numbers — together with the aim, goal, focus sports and notes you entered in the PT intake, so the coaching reflects them (avoid typing anything in the notes you wouldn't want to share with your AI provider, such as health details) — directly from your device to the AI provider you configured in Settings — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or a custom endpoint you specify. That request goes straight from your device to that provider's API using the API key you supplied; it never passes through a Formcast server. That provider's own privacy policy and data-retention terms apply to that request. If you enable Apple's on-device model instead, nothing leaves your device at all for that request.

When you save an API key, and about once a day afterwards, Formcast asks that provider (directly, with your key) which models your account can use, so the app can pick one for you and adopt newer ones automatically. That request contains only your key — no health data and no prompt text. If you install the Formcast Apple Watch app, the iPhone app sends it a small summary — your readiness score, today's coach line and planned workout, and this week's totals — directly to your own paired Watch (Apple's WatchConnectivity, no internet or Formcast server involved). If you record a workout with the Watch app, it reads your heart rate, energy and distance from the Watch's sensors and, for outdoor sports, uses the Watch's location only while the workout runs; the workout (with route) is saved to Apple Health on your devices and nowhere else. While a Watch workout runs, the Watch also sends its live numbers (time, heart rate, energy, distance and, outdoors, your current position) to your paired iPhone through Apple's workout mirroring; nothing is sent anywhere else. The summary is stored on the Watch, shared only with Formcast's watch-face complication through a Keychain group on that Watch, and removed when you delete the Watch app. Formcast also keeps a small usage tally on your device — how many AI calls you made per day and per model, and the token counts your provider reports back — to show an estimate of this month's usage and cost in Settings and to enforce the optional daily call limit. It never leaves your device, it's cleared by "Reset counters" or "Delete everything", and it contains no health data or prompt text.

You can export your data from Settings → Your data → Export data (activities, daily metrics, sleep, HRV, strength sets, planned workouts and, optionally, coach messages) as CSV or JSON. The file is created on your device and handed to the iOS share sheet; it goes only where you choose to send it, never to Formcast. A "detailed export" (Garmin accounts) requests the datasets you tick — sleep with stages, HRV readings, heart rate through the day, stress, Body Battery, activity files and more — directly from Garmin Connect, using your existing Garmin sign-in, for the period you choose; the result is written into a file on your device and nowhere else.

What's stored in the Keychain

  • Your Garmin Connect session token (if signed in with Garmin)
  • Your Fitbit/Google OAuth access and refresh tokens (if signed in with Fitbit)
  • Your AI provider API key (if you've added one)

All of these are stored using iOS's Keychain, encrypted at rest by the OS, and are never transmitted anywhere by Formcast except directly to Garmin, Google, or your chosen AI provider as part of making the requests above.

Deleting your data

Formcast has no account to delete, because it never created one on any server. Everything it knows about you lives on your device, and you can erase all of it at any time from Settings → Your data → Delete everything — this signs you out of Garmin and/or Fitbit, removes your stored API key(s), and wipes every synced activity, health record, and plan from local storage. Uninstalling the app has the same effect. It also leaves all your friends circles, so what you shared there is removed from iCloud too (a circle you made is deleted for everyone in it) — see "Friends circles" above.

Children

Formcast is not directed at children and is not knowingly used to collect data from anyone under 13.
